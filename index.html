The core of the club

Open weekly programming

Every week we host one session any student may walk into. The format rotates, so a member with a narrow interest still finds several sessions a semester that are squarely for them.

01

Hands-on workshops

Rapid prototyping, AI-assisted development, cloud deployment, and product validation. You leave having built the thing, not having watched someone build it.

02

Founders & practitioners

Talks from student founders, alumni entrepreneurs, and people who do this for a living — what actually worked, what it cost, what they'd skip.

03

Open project nights

Members show what they're working on and find collaborators across disciplines. The members' own work is the content of the meeting.

The ladder of participation

Everyone welcome is a design requirement, not a slogan. Each rung is optional and each is reachable from the one below.

  1. Attend a session
  2. Share at a project night
  3. Join a cohort
  4. Return as a mentor
  5. Lead a committee
  6. Run for the board

The deeper track

The six-week cohort

Each semester, members ready to commit to shipping join a small, deliberately cross-disciplinary team — technical members paired with members responsible for the user and the market. Teams work to weekly milestones and finish in front of an audience.

The cohort is not a prerequisite for membership and not the only way to participate. It's the track available to members who want a deadline.

  1. IntakeTeams of 3–4 and a short commitment agreement. Nobody is anonymous.
  2. Weeks 1–5Weekly milestones and short, structured check-ins that are public within the cohort.
  3. Week 6Dry run with officers, then a live demo at the Builder Showcase.
  4. AfterReferrals onward, and an invitation back as a mentor for the next cohort.

Open to the entire campus. Cohort teams present working products on the main stage; the exhibition floor is open to any member to table a project at any stage — personal projects, class projects, research tools, unfinished prototypes.

Feedback, not a leaderboard

Teams receive structured feedback from a panel of faculty, alumni founders, and campus entrepreneurship staff rather than a competitive ranking. The goal is to move projects forward, not to name a winner.

Projects don't end at Demo Day. We maintain a referral pathway to Stony Brook's existing entrepreneurship programs and to outside accelerators, competitions, and early-stage funding.

Why this club

We're not a lecture club. We're a build club.

The constraint has moved

AI-assisted development means a two-person undergrad team can produce in six weeks what recently took a funded team a semester. The limit isn't engineering capacity anymore — it's structure and collaborators.

A shipped product is the output

Technical clubs produce skill. Pre-professional clubs produce placement. We treat execution and viability as one joint requirement, and the deliverable is a product the students own.

Upstream of the incubators

Campus programs serve ventures that already exist. We produce what they need as an input: an undergraduate team with a working prototype and enough traction to be worth advising.

Resources

What members get

SBUILD intends to operate as an affiliated AWS Student Builder Group. The affiliation provides technical resources at no cost to members or to the university.

SBUILD is vendor-neutral by design. The mission, weekly programming, cohort, Showcase, and referral pathway are not conditional on this relationship and continue unchanged on any cloud provider — or none.

  • Cloud credits for member projects
  • Certification vouchers for AWS exams
  • Technical community events beyond campus
  • Mentor access from returning cohort finishers
  • Referrals to campus and outside programs

Before you ask

Common questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Sessions require no technical background, and cohort teams are built to pair technical members with members who own the user and the market. A room of only engineers produces demos without users.

Is there an application?

None. No application, no prerequisite, no interview. If you're a Stony Brook student and you're in the room, you're a member.

Do I have to join a project?

No. You can attend a single session and nothing else. The cohort is opt-in for members who want a deadline.

What if I don't have an idea?

Project nights exist partly for this. Show up, hear what people are working on, and join something. Plenty of members start as a collaborator on someone else's project.

What year can I join?

Any. First-years are explicitly welcome — the club exists in part because a first-year with an idea and no team has no obvious entry point anywhere else.

Come to one session.

That's the whole ask. Leave your email and we'll tell you when and where.
